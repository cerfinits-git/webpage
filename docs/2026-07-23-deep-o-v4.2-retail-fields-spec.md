# DEEP+O v4.2 — Retail Field Spec

- Date: 2026-07-23
- Track: Product / Cerfinits Research
- Status: DRAFT for review — paste into the v4.2 prompt once confirmed
- Purpose: close the gap between what a DEEP+O report emits and what the retail
  dashboard renders (`docs/2026-07-23-research-platform-pivot-grill.md`)

## Why this exists

The dashboard has eight modules. Six are filled: Overview, Financial Health,
Valuation, Growth and Dividend come from EDGAR plus the report's `quality` and
`valuation.ev`. Two are empty because the report emits nothing that can fill
them, and one more is only half-served:

| Module | Needs | Today |
|---|---|---|
| 2 — What the company does | business description, revenue mix, moat, strengths, weaknesses, competitors | **nothing** |
| 7 — Risks | plain-language business risks | `killers` exist, but they are position-exit triggers, not business risks |
| 8 — What kind of stock | descriptive characteristics | derived in code, see §4 — **no prompt change** |

Sections 1 and 2 below are the additions. Most of section 1 is not new analysis:
the Quality score's D and E components cannot be assigned without already having
answered what the business does, what protects it and who it competes with. The
report scores that reasoning and then discards it. This exposes it.

---

## 1. New report section — Business Profile

Emitted as report section **2A**, immediately after Variant Perception, and
transcribed into the registry as `business`.

```ts
business: {
  /** What the company sells, in two sentences a non-accountant understands. */
  whatItDoes: Translatable;

  /**
   * Revenue split from the 10-K's segment reporting. 2–5 entries, shares
   * rounded to whole percent and summing to 100 ± 2. Where one line dominates,
   * say so — concentration is the point of this field.
   */
  revenueMix: { label: Translatable; sharePct: number }[];

  /** What stops a competitor taking the business, and how long it holds. */
  moat: Translatable;
  /** Classification behind the D component of Quality. */
  moatStrength: "wide" | "narrow" | "none";

  /** 2–3 each, one clause per entry, ≤ 90 characters. */
  strengths: Translatable[];
  weaknesses: Translatable[];

  /** 2–5 competitor names as an ordinary reader would recognise them. */
  competitors: string[];
}
```

`moatStrength` belongs inside the Business Summary body. It is **not** a third
headline score — R6 fixes the page at two axes, quality and valuation, and a
third badge beside them would undo that.

## 2. New report section — Business Risks

Emitted as report section **9A**, alongside the Thesis Dashboard, and
transcribed as `businessRisks`.

```ts
businessRisks: {
  /** What could go wrong, ≤ 90 characters, no metric thresholds. */
  risk: Translatable;
  /** Why it matters to someone holding for years, ≤ 140 characters. */
  why: Translatable;
  kind:
    | "concentration"   // one product, customer, region or channel carries the company
    | "regulation"      // law, antitrust, licensing, tax
    | "competition"     // a rival or a substitute
    | "execution"       // the company's own plan, spending or management
    | "cyclical"        // demand rises and falls with the economy
    | "financial";      // debt, refinancing, cash generation
}[]   // 3–4 entries, no two sharing a `kind`
```

**This does not replace `killers`.** Those stay exactly as they are and stay on
the Advanced page. The two answer different questions:

| | `killers` | `businessRisks` |
|---|---|---|
| Question | when does the thesis break | what could go wrong with this company |
| Reader | someone holding, with an exit rule | someone deciding whether to read on |
| Form | metric + threshold + window | plain sentence |
| Example | `Operating margin <35% สองไตรมาสติด` | `รายได้เกือบทั้งหมดมาจากโฆษณาแหล่งเดียว` |

Requiring distinct `kind` values forces coverage: a list of four financial
thresholds is what the current `killers` already give, and repeating it here
adds nothing.

## 3. Writing rules for both sections

These strings reach a retail surface unedited apart from translation, so the
locked constraints apply at the point of writing:

1. **No buy / sell / hold / wait / accumulate**, and nothing that implies it —
   "น่าสนใจ", "คุ้มค่า", "ควรรอ" included (R11.1).
2. **Never describe who the stock suits.** Describe the company, not the reader
   (R11.2).
3. **No unexplained jargon.** The persona does not know PE, PBV, ROE, EBITDA,
   CAGR or moat. Either avoid the term or define it inline in the same sentence.
4. **Every figure traceable to a filing.** Percentages in `revenueMix` come from
   segment reporting, not estimation.
5. **Thai is the source text.** English is a translation of it, not a separate
   composition — the two must state the same facts.
6. Present tense, no hedging stacks ("อาจจะพอ...ในระดับหนึ่ง"). One claim per
   sentence.

## 4. Module 8 stays out of the prompt

"What kind of stock" is computed in code from data already held, so it is
auditable and needs no review cycle — which is what R9 asks for:

| Characteristic | Computed from |
|---|---|
| growth vs mature | 5-year revenue CAGR (EDGAR) |
| pays a steady dividend | length of `dividendPerShare` (EDGAR) |
| capital-hungry | capex ÷ revenue (EDGAR) |
| earnings swing | dispersion of `netProfit` (EDGAR) |
| price swings | dispersion of daily closes (`getPriceHistory`) |

**One pipeline change is required:** `scripts/build-fundamentals.mjs` fetches
capex to derive free cash flow and then discards it. It must be written into the
snapshot as its own series.

## 5. Consistency check — new step before publication

The Quality score is the analyst's judgement; the Financial Health lights are
computed from EDGAR by rule. Nothing currently reconciles them, so a page can
show five stars above a debt light reading "น่ากังวล" — sometimes correct (a
strong business borrowing through a build cycle), sometimes a stale report.

Add to the report, as the last item before the verdict, the four lights the rule
engine will produce, computed by the same thresholds the code uses:

| Light | Rule (must match `lib/research/dashboard.ts`) |
|---|---|
| Revenue | grew more than **2%** in a majority of the last 3 years → good; shrank in a majority → poor; otherwise watch |
| Net profit | same rule as revenue |
| Cash flow | free cash flow positive in all 3 years → good, **unless** the latest year fell **40%** or more → watch; negative in a majority → poor |
| Debt | net debt ÷ operating cash flow < **2** → good; ≤ **4** → watch; above → poor; net cash → good |

Then state one line: whether the Quality score contradicts any light, and if it
does, why. That explanation belongs in `business.weaknesses` or
`businessRisks`, where the reader will actually see it.
